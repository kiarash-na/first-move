# Hackathon Challenge | Business Models for 5GDHC

## 1 Challenge Overview

How 5GDHC networks move from technically promising concepts to viable, scalable and sustainable business models?

During this hackathon, interdisciplinary teams will develop, test and refine a business model for a concrete 5th Generation District Heating and Cooling (5GDHC / anergy network) use case. The emphasis is on customers, value creation, ownership, financing and implementation—not detailed engineering design.

Of particular note here is the scalability of the models.

## 2 Goals

- Discuss, challenge and further develop a Business Model Canvas tailored to 5GDHC.
- Assess the practical applicability and economic plausibility of implementation models.
- Optimize business models through expert input, mentoring and iterative feedback.
- Identify credible pathways towards sustainable, real-world implementation of 5GDHC Networks.
- Identify the potential for project replication and scalability

## 3 The Problem

Technical feasibility alone does not secure a successful 5GDHC project. Projects must address a real demand, offer a convincing alternative to individual heating and cooling solutions, coordinate multiple stakeholders and remain viable over the long term.

Key questions include:

- Who benefits and who pays?
- Who owns and operates the network?
- What motivates customers to connect?
- How are investments, revenues and risks distributed?
- How do electricity prices, funding, regulation and long-term commitments affect feasibility?
- How can we develop and scale business models here?

## 4 The Challenge

Develop and pitch focused, evidence-informed business model canvas for a 5GDHC network based on one concrete scenario. Teams should formulate a clear value proposition, identify the relevant stakeholders and customers, outline a realistic operating and financing approach, and test the most important commercial assumptions. Solutions may include innovative partnerships, tariff structures, ownership models or approaches to demand aggregation.

### Possible use cases

Create multiple Business Model Canvases here. What works, what doesn't, and where are the opportunities for scalability?

- Existing neighbourhood or district refurbishment: connecting multiple buildings with differing heating and cooling needs.
- Waste-heat integration: using industrial, commercial or other local low-temperature heat sources.
- Rural or small-town applications: making low-density networks commercially and organizationally feasible.
- Teams may select a provided scenario or propose a comparable use case, subject to challenge-track alignment.

### Expected Outcomes:

- A concise definition of the use case: target audience, problem statement, needs, and local context.
- Work with the Business Model Canvas—analysis based on the Business Model Canvas
- What works? What doesn’t work? Can these models be scaled? If so, where? If not, why not?
- A simplified assessment of feasibility and implementability: costs/revenue, operating and ownership models, financing, and incentives for stakeholders.
- Final presentation

## 6 Working Method & Evaluation

Suggested development steps - but it’s up to you

### 1 | Define the opportunity

Describe the site or district, customer segments, heating/cooling needs, relevant heat sources and the key pain point. Explain why a shared low-temperature network offers value compared with available alternatives.

### 2 | Build the business logic

Complete a tailored Business Model Canvas, with particular attention to value proposition, customer relationships, key partners, operator responsibilities, cost structure and revenue streams. Make the allocation of investment, risk and benefits explicit.

### 3 | Run the Reality Check

Use rough but transparent assumptions rather than detailed engineering calculations. Test plausible connection rates, tariffs, capital expenditure, financing and subsidy dependencies. Identify the Top 3 risks and Top 3 critical assumptions, including one potential deal-breaker. Explain how each could be validated.

### 4 | Improve & prepare implementation

Incorporate mentor feedback into the models – work on different Canvas, develop them. Set out the next practical steps: data to collect, stakeholders to engage, partnerships to secure, pilot opportunities and a realistic decision gate for progressing the concept.

### Final pitch

- Use case and customer problem: who needs the solution and why?
- Proposed 5GDHC solution and value proposition.
- Business model: customers, partners, operator, financing and revenue logic.
- Practical viability: why it could work and which assumptions support it.
- Biggest uncertainty, key risks and next validation steps.
- Proposed jury evaluation criteria
- Problem–solution fit and clarity of the use case.
- Business-model coherence and stakeholder incentives.
- Economic plausibility and realistic implementation pathway.
- Quality of risk analysis, assumptions and validation approach.
- Innovation, scalability and clarity of the final pitch.
