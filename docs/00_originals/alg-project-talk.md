## Slide 5: Organisational Learnings

- **Most challenges are organisational rather than technical**: Coordination, ownership structures, financing and long-term operation are key factors
- **Early alignment with municipalities and authorities is essential**: Spatial planning, permits and local strategies strongly influence feasibility
- **Ownership structures determine implementation pathways**: Different models are needed for utilities, housing associations and private owners
- **Local leadership and committed initiators are critical**: Successful projects require active coordination and long-term commitment
- **Scalable business and governance models are needed**: Standardised implementation models can reduce complexity and accelerate replication

## Slide 6: What Is an Anergy/5GDHC Network?

**Low-Temperature Network**
5th-generation heating and cooling network at **10–25 °C**, so **losses are minimal**.
**Sources:** groundwater, geothermal, waste heat, wastewater

**Heat Pumps in Each Building**
**Decentralised heat pumps** raise the temperature to what each building needs, for radiators or floor heating.
**Challenge:** higher upfront investment and more complex control.

**Heating and Cooling**
Energy flows **in both directions**: when some buildings heat and others cool, their demands **balance each other**.
**Benefit:** summer cooling becomes a reason to connect.

## Slide 8: Examples in Practice

| Example | Description | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Viertel Zwei, Vienna** | Residential, commercial and public buildings with **decentralised heat pumps** in Vienna’s 2nd district. | **Sources:** geothermal, wastewater, groundwater. In operation since 2017, beyond carbon energy. |
| **Bregenz, Vorarlberg** | Water from **Lake Constance** as the source for heating and cooling, run by the **municipal utility**. | **Operator:** Stadtwerke Bregenz. In operation, network being expanded. |
| **Lagarde, Bamberg** | Existing and new buildings, **approx. 1,200 dwellings**, 70 decentralised heat pumps, 5.5 km of pipe. | **Sources:** geothermal, wastewater heat. In operation since 2023, Stadtwerke Bamberg. |

## Slide 9: Learnings from Our Use Cases

- **Existing buildings** can be integrated: Most even without major thermal renovation
- **Heat line density** decides the economics: Above approx. 1 MWh per metre of trench, networks can beat individual solutions
- **Pipes and house connections** drive the cost: Trench length dominates the investment
- **Groundwater** is the most economical source: Micro-networks between direct neighbours are a promising option

## Slide 10: Tips for the Challenge

**Good to Know**

- **Costs come first:** investment upfront, revenues over 25–30 years.
- **Every building that stays out** raises costs for the rest.
- **Extra value:** cooling, stable prices, grid relief.

**Framework Conditions**

- **Many owners** decide individually: homes, housing associations, public buildings.
- **Connecting is voluntary:** no fixed gas phase-out date yet.
- **Funding** fits only partly and depends on who owns what.

**What Helps**

- **System boundary:** who owns source, network, heat pumps?
- **Fair cost key:** e.g. source by connected load, pipes by those who use them.
- **Start small:** anchor customers, modular growth, micro-networks.
- **Think infrastructure:** long-term finance, public land, the city as partner.
