# Hackathon Challenge | Business Models for 5GDHC

## Overview and Scope

How can 5th Generation District Heating and Cooling (5GDHC) networks evolve from technically promising concepts into economically viable, scalable and sustainable business models?

During this hackathon, interdisciplinary teams will develop, test and refine business models for 5GDHC networks. The focus is on customer needs, value creation, ownership structures, financing and practical implementation rather than detailed engineering design.

Particular attention will be given to different geographical and settlement conditions, including existing neighbourhoods, rural areas and locations.

A key objective is to explore the scalability and replicability of different business models.

## Context

A 5GDHC Network is a low-temperature system that supplies buildings with heating and cooling. Water circulates at around 5-25°C and therefore enables an efficient use of renewable energy and waste heat without requiring insulated pipes. The network connects buildings with energy sources and thermal storage systems, such as borehole fields, buffer tanks, or seasonal storage. Each building uses a heat pump to provide heating or cooling. A key advantage is the bidirectional energy exchange: excess heat can be fed back into the network and used by other buildings, improving overall energy efficiency and sustainability.

Beyond increased sustainability, 5GDHC networks can also contribute to a resilient, secure and locally available energy supply due to the integration of local energy sources, reduced dependence on individual energy carriers and flexible thermal energy exchange and storage.

5GDHC networks can be implemented in different settlement and building typologies and require suitable technical and economic conditions. Practical tools can support municipalities, energy service providers and other actors in identifying suitable areas and assessing their technical and economic potential.

Building on the findings and insights gained from the research project "Anergie Leicht Gemacht", this hackathon aims to explore and develop viable, scalable and replicable business models for the practical implementation of 5GDHC networks.

## Challenge Description and Problem Statement

Technical feasibility alone does not secure a successful 5GDHC project. Projects must address a real demand, offer a convincing alternative to individual heating and cooling solutions, coordinate multiple stakeholders and remain viable over the long term.

Key questions include:

Who benefits and who pays? Who owns and operates the network? What motivates customers to connect? How are investments, revenues and risks distributed? How do energy prices, funding, regulation and long-term commitments affect feasibility? How can 5GDHC business models contribute to resilient local energy infrastructure and energy security? What risks, dependencies and operational responsibilities need to be considered to ensure a reliable and secure energy supply?

Challenge Objectives:

- Develop and critically assess business models tailored to 5GDHC networks.
- Evaluate their economic feasibility, practical applicability and implementation potential.
- Refine business models through expert input, mentoring and iterative feedback.
- Identify key opportunities, barriers and critical success factors.
- Explore the scalability and replicability of promising business models across different geographical and organizational contexts

## The Challenge

Teams are challenged to develop and present a convincing, evidence-based business model for a 5GDHC network based on a specific use case.

The focus is on creating a realistic value proposition, identifying relevant stakeholders, developing suitable ownership and financing structures, and critically assessing economic feasibility and implementation potential.

Teams may develop a new business model, refine an existing approach, or compare different models to identify the most promising solution.

Particular attention should be given to scalability, replicability and the conditions required for successful implementation.

Teams can choose from predefined scenarios or propose their own use case aligned with the challenge objectives.

Possible use cases: Here, you can develop various Business Model Canvases, compare them, refine them, or find new solutions. What works, what doesn’t, and where are the opportunities for scalability? Predefined scenarios are available, and you can also develop your own.

The following points may be taken into consideration:

- Existing neighbourhood or district refurbishment: connecting multiple buildings with differing heating and cooling needs.
- Waste-heat integration: using industrial, commercial or other local low-temperature heat sources.
- Applications in rural areas or on the outskirts of cities: Economic and organizational feasibility of low-density networks.
- Teams may select a provided scenario or propose a comparable use case, subject to challenge-track alignment.

## Working method & evaluation

Suggested development steps - but it’s up to you

### 1 | Define the opportunity

Describe the site or district, customer segments, heating/cooling needs, relevant heat sources and the key pain point. Explain why a shared low-temperature network offers value compared with available alternatives.

### 2 | Build the business logic

Complete a tailored Business Model Canvas, with particular attention to value proposition, customer relationships, key partners, operator responsibilities, cost structure and revenue streams. Make the allocation of investment, risk and benefits explicit.

### 3 | Run the Reality Check

Use rough but transparent assumptions rather than detailed engineering calculations. Test plausible connection rates, tariffs, capital expenditure, financing and subsidy dependencies. Identify the top risks and top critical assumptions, including one potential deal-breaker. Explain how each could be validated.

### 4 | Improve & prepare implementation

Refine your business model based on the Reality Check, expert feedback and identified challenges. Assess its potential for scalability and replication under different geographical, economic and organisational conditions. Outline the next practical steps, including data requirements, stakeholder engagement, potential partnerships, pilot opportunities and key decisions needed to move towards implementation.

## Final Pitch

Teams should present their proposed business model and explain why it represents a promising approach for their selected 5GDHC use case.

The final pitch should address the following aspects:

- Use Case & Value Proposition: What problem does the model solve, for whom, and why is a 5GDHC network a suitable solution?
- Business Model: How does the model create and capture value? Who are the key stakeholders, customers and partners?
- Economic & Practical Feasibility: How are investment, ownership, operation, financing, revenues and risks addressed?
- Reality Check: What are the critical assumptions, challenges and potential deal-breakers? What works, what doesn't, and why?
- Scalability & Replicability: Can the model be expanded or transferred to other locations? Under which conditions?
- Next Steps: What would be required to move towards real-world implementation?

## Expected Outcome

The goal is to develop the most convincing and feasible business model, considering not only its economic viability but also its scalability, replicability and real-world implementation potential.

Equally important is the critical assessment of opportunities, challenges and limitations. Teams should clearly identify what works, what doesn't, and why, supporting their conclusions with well-founded arguments.

A successful outcome is not necessarily a perfect business model, but a convincing, realistic and critically evaluated approach that demonstrates a clear understanding of its implementation potential and limitations.
